AI Applications

Artificial Intelligence (AI) enables machines to simulate human intelligence — learning, reasoning, and problem-solving. It is transforming every sector from healthcare to entertainment.

Learning Objectives

  • 12.4.3.1 Describe spheres where AI is applied: industry, education, medicine, gaming, society
  • 12.4.3.2 Explain the purpose of virtual and augmented reality

Conceptual Anchor

The Digital Brain Analogy

AI is like giving a computer a brain — not a biological one, but a mathematical one. Just as our brain learns from experience (touching a hot stove = pain → avoid), AI learns from data (1,000 cat photos → recognise cats). The more data, the "smarter" the brain becomes.

Rules & Theory

AI Applications by Sector

Sector Application Examples
Industry Automation, quality control, predictive maintenance Robot assembly lines, defect detection cameras
Education Personalised learning, automated grading, tutoring Khan Academy AI, ChatGPT, adaptive tests
Medicine Diagnosis, drug discovery, patient monitoring AI X-ray analysis, robotic surgery, wearable sensors
Gaming NPC behaviour, procedural generation, playtesting Chess engines (Stockfish), AI opponents, world generation
Society Smart cities, voice assistants, autonomous vehicles Siri/Alexa, self-driving cars, traffic prediction

Types of AI

Type Description Example
Narrow AI (Weak) Designed for one specific task Spam filter, chess engine
General AI (Strong) Can perform any intellectual task a human can (theoretical) Does not exist yet
Machine Learning AI that learns from data without being explicitly programmed Netflix recommendations
Expert System AI that uses rules to make decisions in a specific domain Medical diagnosis system

Virtual Reality (VR) vs Augmented Reality (AR)

Feature Virtual Reality (VR) Augmented Reality (AR)
Definition Fully immersive, computer-generated environment Digital elements overlaid on the real world
Hardware VR headset (Oculus, HTC Vive) Phone camera, AR glasses (HoloLens)
Real world visible? No — you only see the virtual world Yes — you see real world + digital overlay
Examples VR gaming, flight simulators, virtual tours Pokémon GO, IKEA Place app, Snapchat filters
Use in education Virtual lab experiments, historical simulations 3D anatomy models, interactive textbooks
Use in medicine Surgical training, phobia treatment Surgical guidance overlays

Ethical Concerns of AI

AI raises important questions: Job displacement (automation replacing workers), bias (AI trained on biased data makes biased decisions), privacy (facial recognition surveillance), and accountability (who's responsible when AI makes errors?).

Common Pitfalls

Confusing VR and AR

VR replaces the real world entirely. AR adds to the real world. If you can still see your room, it's AR. If you're completely in another world, it's VR.

Self-Check Quiz

Q1: What is the difference between Narrow AI and General AI?

Narrow AI is designed for one specific task (e.g., spam filter). General AI would perform any intellectual task like a human — it doesn't exist yet.

Q2: Give an example of AI in gaming.

Chess engines (Stockfish, AlphaZero), AI-controlled NPCs, procedural world generation.

Q3: Can you see the real world in VR?

No — VR is fully immersive; you only see the computer-generated environment. AR overlays digital content on the real world.
