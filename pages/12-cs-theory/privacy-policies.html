Privacy Policies

A privacy policy is a legal document that explains how an organization collects, stores, uses, and shares personal data. Understanding privacy policies is essential in a world where data is the "new oil".

Learning Objectives

  • 12.1.3.3 Explain privacy policies
  • 12.1.3.4 Analyze problems arising due to disseminating and using information

Conceptual Anchor

The Rental Agreement

When you rent an apartment, the landlord explains: what they'll provide, what they might inspect, and what happens with your deposit. A privacy policy is a similar "contract" between you and a website: they tell you what data they collect, why, and who sees it.

Rules & Theory

What Privacy Policies Cover

Section What It Explains
Data collected Name, email, location, browsing history, cookies
Purpose Why data is needed: improving service, advertising, legal compliance
Storage & retention Where data is stored, how long it's kept
Third-party sharing Whether data is shared with advertisers, partners, governments
User rights Right to access, correct, or delete personal data
Security measures How data is protected (encryption, firewalls)

Key Data Protection Laws

Law/Regulation Region Key Point
GDPR European Union Strictest data protection law; right to be forgotten; heavy fines
Kazakhstan Data Protection Law Kazakhstan Personal data requires consent; citizens have access rights
COPPA USA Protects children under 13; parental consent needed

Problems from Disseminating Information

Problem Description Example
Identity theft Personal data used to impersonate someone Stolen ID used to open bank accounts
Cyberbullying Sharing private info/images to harass someone Posting embarrassing photos without consent
Fake news / misinformation False information spread as truth Health misinformation during a pandemic
Data profiling Creating detailed profiles for manipulation Targeted political advertising based on browsing habits
Loss of reputation Personal information taken out of context Old social media posts resurfacing

Digital Footprint

Every action online leaves a trace — your digital footprint. This includes social media posts, search history, online purchases, and more. Once data is online, it can be nearly impossible to fully remove.

Common Pitfalls

"I Have Nothing to Hide"

Privacy isn't about hiding — it's about control. You close the bathroom door not because you're doing something wrong, but because it's your right. The same applies to personal data.

Tasks

Remember

List 5 things a privacy policy should include.

Apply

Read the privacy policy of a social media platform you use. Summarize what data they collect and who they share it with.

Analyze

A data breach exposes 10,000 students' personal records. Analyze the problems this could cause for the students.

Self-Check Quiz

Q1: What is a privacy policy?

A legal document explaining how an organization collects, stores, uses, and shares personal data.

Q2: Name 3 problems caused by sharing personal information online.

Identity theft, cyberbullying, data profiling, fake news/misinformation, loss of reputation.

Q3: What is GDPR?

The General Data Protection Regulation — the EU's strict data protection law giving citizens rights over their personal data, with heavy fines for violations.
